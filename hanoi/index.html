How it works

The rules

  1. There are three pegs. All the discs start stacked on the first peg, smallest on top.
  2. You may move only one disc at a time, and only the top disc of a peg.
  3. You may never put a bigger disc on a smaller disc.
  4. You win when the whole stack is on the third peg.

The recursive idea

To move n discs from peg A to peg C (using B as a helper):

  1. Move the top n - 1 discs from A to B.
  2. Move the largest disc from A to C.
  3. Move the n - 1 discs from B onto C.

Steps 1 and 3 are the same puzzle again, just with one disc fewer. This goes on until there is only one disc, which can simply be moved. That easy case is called the base case.

move(n, A, C, B):
  if n = 0: stop            (base case)
  move(n - 1, A, B, C)      (clear the way)
  move disc n from A to C
  move(n - 1, B, C, A)      (rebuild on top)

How many moves? 2n - 1

Let M(n) be the fewest moves for n discs. We move the small tower twice and the big disc once: M(n) = 2 × M(n - 1) + 1. That gives M(n) = 2n - 1. Every extra disc doubles the work (plus one).

Link to algorithms and recursion

  • Recursion: a function that solves a big problem by calling itself on a smaller copy of the problem, with a base case to stop.
  • Divide and conquer: split the job into smaller jobs, solve them, and combine the answers.
  • Exponential growth: the number of steps doubles with each disc. With 64 discs, at one move per second, the job would take about 585 billion years! Problems whose work explodes like this are hard for even the fastest computers.
  • Hint and Auto-solve: they use the same idea, but start from wherever the discs are now. For the biggest disc that is not yet on its goal peg, first clear the smaller discs onto the spare peg, then move it.
